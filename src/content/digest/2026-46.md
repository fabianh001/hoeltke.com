Leadership changes at the chip supplier, a lab heading to public markets, and an on-device classifier nobody documented. The week's news is mostly about who controls AI systems and what they reveal.

## Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO after 33 years

Huang resigned on November 12, with Colette Kress taking over as interim CEO. The departure followed a Wall Street Journal report that Nvidia was preparing to oust him and a letter from Huang to staff about an affair; employees have since petitioned for his return.

**Why it matters:** Nvidia's accelerators underpin most large-scale AI training, so a change at the top of the main supplier affects compute plans well beyond one company. How the interim leadership handles Nvidia's open-source work and its US policy posture is now unsettled. ([Jensen Huang resigns](https://apnews.com/article/nvidia-megaspeed-china-singapore-huang-5403f152a601cf82ce939f1d24fffa0f))

## Anthropic launches roadshow for proposed IPO

Anthropic, PBC began the roadshow for a proposed initial public offering of Class A common stock on November 10. Simon Willison posted the roadshow video the next day.

**Why it matters:** A public listing would put a major model developer in front of ordinary investors and force it to disclose numbers that labs have kept private. That makes it the clearest look yet at how the economics of frontier AI actually work. ([Anthropic launches roadshow for proposed initial public offering](https://www.anthropic.com/news/roadshow-launch))

## Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2

Anthropic calls the two models its most capable for coding, finance, and scientific work; Latent Space describes Fable 5.2 as retaking the top spot among LLMs. A developer blog post describes a team of 1,000 agents running an early build of Fable 5.2 reproducing a formal proof of Fermat's Last Theorem with general tools, a shared database, and layered safety controls.

**Why it matters:** The interesting part isn't only the model but the coordination around it: many agents sharing state, with limits on what each can do. Anthropic also published a guide to choosing between Fable, Opus, Sonnet, and Haiku and what each costs, which is the practical question for anyone paying per token. ([Introducing Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2))

## Goldman Sachs pulls Claude Code from bankers and traders

Goldman Sachs is removing Claude Code from its bankers and traders, according to The State of AI.

**Why it matters:** Enterprise adoption is not a one-way ratchet: a marquee deployment can be reversed, and access is not the same as acceptance. Anyone reading a big-name rollout as durable demand should treat it as provisional. ([Goldman Sachs is pulling Claude Code from its bankers and traders](https://www.thestateofai.com/news/goldman-sachs-claude-code-traders-bankers))

## Every Mac ships with an undocumented family member classifier

An analysis at 8rank.ai reports that Apple Intelligence builds a personal knowledge graph on the device, including an undocumented classifier that infers family relationships from ordinary data. The inference happens locally rather than in the cloud.

**Why it matters:** On-device personalization can derive sensitive facts — who counts as your family — from data you never supplied for that purpose, and the derivation may not be visible to you. For anyone building on Apple's on-device intelligence, what the system infers and what users can inspect is worth checking. ([Every Mac comes with an (undocumented) family member classifier](https://8rank.ai/game/apple-intelligence-personal-knowledge-graph))
